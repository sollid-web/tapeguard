TapeGuardian · an autonomous agent with rules you set

Your crypto can work for you at 3am — without you handing over the keys.

TapeGuardian is an autonomous agent — think of a robot trader that comes with a rulebook written into the blockchain itself. You decide the limits once: how much it may move per trade, how much per day, which exchange it may use, and how far your balance may drift. The 20-second version

what this is

Problem: to trade automatically, a bot normally needs your private key. That key is unlimited power — one leak drains everything, and you can never prove afterwards what it was allowed to do.

What TapeGuardian does: instead of trusting a key, the rules are enforced by a small program on the blockchain. The bot runs with a key that can only push actions those rules already allow. Steal that key and you still cannot spend more than the daily limit, cannot use a non-approved exchange, and cannot touch the owner's money.

What you get: trading that keeps going while you sleep, provable limits, a stop button in the app, and a second, permanent stop switch on-chain.

Cost: a one-off activation fee (5 USDT in this build) plus a small cut of what it earns, both paid into an account you control.

How it works — four steps, no jargon

1

Write your rules

One screen: maximum per trade, maximum per day, how much the balance may drift from your target, and which exchange address it is allowed to talk to. Anything not on the list is impossible.

2

Fund the agent's account

You send money to an account that belongs to you but has no private key at all. It can only ever move funds through your rulebook, so there is nothing to steal from it.

3

Turn the agent on

A small always-on program (the daemon) checks the market every minute, asks the rulebook "am I allowed to do this?", and only then acts. If nothing is worth doing, it does nothing and logs that too.

4

Watch it, pause it, withdraw it

You see the live balance, every action and every refusal. Pause any time, change the limits any time, and withdraw your funds whenever you like — including the fees it earned for you.

What it will never do

enforced on-chain
  • Move more than your per-trade or per-day limit — the check happens inside the same transaction as the trade.
  • Trade on an exchange or token you did not allow.
  • Spend the fees it collected for you — that money is reserved and can only go to your address.
  • Keep going after you hit Stop, or after its paid period ends.
  • Change the rules to give itself more room — only the owner (your account) can do that.
These are not promises in a document: they are the code the agent runs against, and you can verify each one on-chain.

Words used on this page

tap to open
Rules / "policy circuit"

The rulebook. It is a tiny program on the blockchain, owned by your account, that says yes or no to every action. Because it lives on-chain, nobody — not even the people who built this site — can quietly change it.

Agent

A program that acts on your behalf without you watching. Every trading bot is an agent; what makes this one different is that its limits are enforced by the blockchain rather than by its own good behaviour.

The agent's account / "container"

The wallet that actually holds the money. It has no private key, so it cannot be "hacked" in the usual sense: the only way for money to leave is a call that the rulebook approves.

The agent's always-on part / "daemon"

A small program running on a server, not in your browser. That is the whole point: it keeps working when your phone is off. It only ever decides, asks permission and submits; it cannot change your rules or withdraw.

"Keeper" / "relayer key"

The low-power key the agent uses to send actions. Think of it as a courier: it can carry a package the rulebook approved, it cannot sign a new one, cannot change limits and holds no funds. If it ever leaks, you revoke it and the loss is capped by your daily limit.

Trigger

A reason to act: "OKB dropped below $42", "check every 5 minutes", or "my balance drifted 5% away from my target". No trigger, no action.

"Demo simulation"

When the contracts are not deployed yet, this page runs the whole cycle with a faithful local copy of the rulebook, so you can see exactly how it behaves before spending anything. It is labelled clearly wherever it is active.

Honest expectations

read this before funding
  • It is a trader, not a savings account. Automated trading can lose money on a bad trade; the limits cap how much, they do not make profit.
  • Small on-chain bills. You pay gas to create the rulebook, the account and its wiring, plus the activation fee. Every number is itemised before you confirm.
  • The agent needs a reachable server. Its always-on part runs on a Worker that must be deployed; if it is offline, nothing is lost — actions wait in a queue and are re-checked before they are sent.
  • You are always the owner. Whatever happens to the server, control of the rules and the funds stays with your account.

Rebalancing strategy

Path sell = [wrapped OKB → USDT], path buy = [USDT → wrapped OKB]. Both are built from the two addresses above; the router, the paths and the swap selectors must also be whitelisted on the policy circuit or every trade is rejected on chain.
In plain words. Target weight is how much of your money you want in OKB — 6000 bps means 60% OKB and 40% USDT. Drift band is how far it may wander before the agent acts — 500 bps means "leave it alone until it is 5% off". Max trades per day is a hard brake. Minimum trade stops it wasting fees on dust. Slippage is how much worse than the quoted price a trade may end up before it is refused. You do not have to get these perfect: nothing can exceed them, and you can change them any time.

Deployment, wiring and demo script

Architecture

  • Policy layer — PolicyCircuit: a non-upgradeable rule engine on X Layer whose owner is fixed at deployment, while policy values such as limits, allowlists, and pause state remain owner-configurable. CIRCUIT_ID, a recomputable commitment, pause flag, per-action and daily limits, target + selector whitelists, authorised agents. check() is a pure view, so anyone can dry-run a proposal without spending gas.
  • Execution layer — AgentContainer: the keyless account owned by your TapeOut circuit container. It holds the funds, calls the circuit, records the spend and only then performs the call — simulate() is the free preview of exactly that path.
  • Daemon: an always-on server (Cloudflare Worker, cron trigger or POST /api/tick). It evaluates the rules — drift rebalancing, price thresholds, intervals — asks the circuit, and submits approved actions as an authorised relayer. The relayer holds no funds and cannot withdraw or reconfigure anything, so nobody has to be around.

Deployment on X Layer

  • Deploy PolicyCircuit and AgentContainer (owner = your TapeOut circuit container for both). Record the circuit ID and the container address.
  • One-time wiring, routed through your circuit container (owner-only, the app does the routing): AgentContainer.setPolicy(policy), PolicyCircuit.setAgent(container, true), PolicyCircuit.setLimits(...), setTarget(router, true, false) plus the swap selectors, and AgentContainer.setRelayer(keeper, true) — the Autonomy tab builds that last calldata for you.
  • Deploy the server and add its secrets: ADMIN_PASSWORD, RELAYER_KEY (the keeper), RPC_URL, optionally DAEMON_SECRET and PRICE_URL. Add a cron trigger every minute so the agent runs with nobody present.
  • Publish the page, then set APP_ORIGINS to the URL it is served from (comma-separated if several) and redeploy the server. The Worker refuses browser requests from any other origin instead of answering Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *; /api/health shows whether it is still locked.
  • Set a per-token cap with PolicyCircuit.setTokenLimit(USDT, maxPerAction, dailyLimit) as well as the native limits — a swap carries value = 0, so the native cap alone does not bound the USDT side.
  • Open Autonomy, unlock with the owner password, save the allocation (target weight, drift band, limits, router, USDT, wrapped OKB) and add the rules.
  • Publish the page to DeWEB and keep ?public=1 as the wallet-free status link for judges and users.
